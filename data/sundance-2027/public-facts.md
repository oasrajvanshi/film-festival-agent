# Sundance Film Festival 2027 (Boulder, CO): public facts for seed data

Research date: 2026-11-16/17. Everything below comes from public sources, and each fact has a URL. Labels used:

- **UNCONFIRMED**: the fact is inferred, comes from a single unofficial source, or sources conflict. The reason is given.
- **PRECEDENT (2026 Park City)**: 2027 information is not published yet, so the nearest real equivalent is given instead.
- **COMPUTED**: I derived it, for example a distance from coordinates or a sunrise time from an astronomical formula.

**How the official pages were read.** festival.sundance.org is a React app, and WebFetch returns only `<title>` for it. The page text comes from the site's public WordPress CMS JSON at `https://live-fest-cms.pantheonsite.io/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/<id>` and `/posts/<id>`. I cite the public festival.sundance.org path and the CMS page id, so every quote can be reproduced. The map at festival.sundance.org/media/maps is an iframe of `https://sunins.mcbmap.com/27map`, a Base44 app. Its JS bundle holds fallback venue, stop, parking and hotel data with coordinates. The site's "View Larger Map" link is a Google My Maps layer, exportable as KML: `https://www.google.com/maps/d/kml?mid=1gBefaDctl_LEHW-kH5RspT_iftE&forcekml=1`. Venue coordinates match exactly across both map sources.

**Stale-content warning.** Several official pages still carry 2026 Park City or Salt Lake City text next to the 2027 Boulder text. That includes festival store addresses, the United SLC offer, the pass pickup at Park City's Gateway Center, the volunteer hub at the Sheraton, the Queue-it date "01/12/26", and the sponsors page listing "Festival Host State: State of Utah". I flag these wherever they appear.

**Ticket and pass rules** (Ticketing; Attend in Person; pass guide):

- **All tickets are digital (QR code).** They are available in the "Sundance Film Festival 2027" app ("My Tickets & Passes"), on the web ("My Schedule"), and in Apple or Google Wallet. A printable PDF is an option. The festival issues no physical tickets.
- **Ticket vs. Festival ID.** A ticket admits you to a screening. A Festival ID QR code admits you to events such as Offscreen. The two are separate.
- **Purchase limits.** Packages: 2 per account; extras must be gifted. Ignite: 1 per account. Single tickets: up to 10 per screening, but you keep only 1 and gift the rest.
- **Changes and transfers.** Package holders can change selections up to **4 hours** before a screening. Individual tickets are fully transferable, repeatedly, until used. Whole passes and packages cannot be transferred after purchase; gift them at checkout.
- **Refunds.** All sales are final. If an in-person screening is canceled "due to an act of nature or a technical issue," single-ticket holders get a refund or an exchange. If the whole festival is canceled, everyone is refunded. Processing fees are never refunded.
- **Queue-it.** The online queue opens 15–20 minutes before an on-sale. The listed date "Public Package Fulfillment – 01/12/26" is stale.
- **No reserved seating.** All seating is general admission, first-come, first-seated.
- **Arrival times.** Lines form about **1 hour** before showtime. The house opens about **30 minutes** before. Ticket holders must be in line **15 minutes** before the published start, or admission depends on available seats. Express passholders should arrive **30 minutes** early for priority entry and be in line by T-15 minutes. Groups are seated only when everyone in the party is present.
- **Late entry** is at theater staff's discretion if space is available.
- **Waitlist (e-Waitlist)** (Ticketing):
  - You cannot sign up in person. Use the website or app, with a Festival account.
  - Registration opens **2 hours before showtime** and closes **30 minutes before**.
  - You get a numbered position. You may add one companion, who also gets a number.
  - Empty seats are sold to the waitlist line at the start of the screening.
  - A valid credit or debit card must be on your account.
  - Unused package tickets can be used in the waitlist line.
  - PRECEDENT (2026): volunteers were told to join the waitlist line at the theater 30 minutes before start (Volunteer Hub).
  - Waitlist ticket price: UNCONFIRMED, not stated. It is probably the single-ticket price of $37, but this is not confirmed.
- **Audience voting.** You need a scanned ticket or pass. Vote in the app or on the platform with a 4-star rating. One vote per screening, and votes cannot be changed. A reminder email goes out **1 hour before the scheduled end** of the screening (Attend in Person).
- **Ratings and age.** Most films are unrated. Age-gated 18+ films are flagged, and staff may ask minors for ID.

## 2. Venues

**Official list.** As of the Feb 24, 2026 release (https://www.sundance.org/blogs/2027-sundance-film-festival-to-take-place-january-21-31-in-boulder-colorado/), there are 11 theaters plus 4 talks/programming venues. The later venues page (CMS 26666) also names **Grace Gamm Theater and Vandeveer Workshop** at the Dairy. Denver7 reported **"23 screens… across 14 venues within about two miles"**, up from 16 screens in Park City (https://www.denver7.com/news/local-news/boulder-braces-for-sundance-film-festival-as-passes-go-on-sale-and-homeowners-rush-to-host). How the 23 screens break down is UNCONFIRMED; it presumably includes several Cinemark auditoriums.

**Geography.**
- "Our venues are all within about a 2-mile radius" (What to Know post).
- The four CU venues are "within a 10-minute walk" of one another (https://festival.sundance.org/highlights/inside-the-2027-sundance-film-festival-venues-across-boulders-campuses).
- The map's default centre is 40.009424896537595, -105.27780137037486, zoom 13 (CMS 15806).

**Coordinates.** These come from the official map data. The mcbmap bundle and the Google My Maps KML agree to 7 decimal places.

**Upgrades.** A **$16 million** bond package, a low-cost CECFA loan to Sundance Institute, funds audio-visual and accessibility upgrades at **10 venues** (https://www.kunc.org/news/2026-02-27/sundance-funds-upgrades-to-10-boulder-venues-in-advance-of-film-festival). Visit Boulder describes the same work as "about $16 million in upgrades to local theaters and performance spaces, including school auditoriums" (https://www.bouldercoloradousa.com/sundance-film-festival/festival-essentials/faqs/).

- Officials estimate about **85,000 visitors** (KUNC, Mar 20, 2026); about 85,000 expected (CBS Colorado, June 25, 2026: https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/sundance-film-festival-boulder-balance-high-rental-prices-affordable-lodging-options/).

UNCONFIRMED; this comes from a search snippet of https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2025/01/30/bipartisan-bill-offers-34-million-in-tax-credits-to-attract-sundance-film-festival-to-colorado/.

### 5.3 Known risks

- **Upslope snowstorms.** A low passing south of the Front Range drives easterly, upslope flow, and Boulder often gets the heaviest totals. Past events produced 20–35 in in Boulder and Larimer counties. Source: BoulderCAST, a private local forecaster (https://bouldercast.com/storm-recap-did-you-get-one-two-or-three-feet-of-snow/), via search snippet; secondary source.
- **Chinook / downslope windstorms.** January is a peak month. The PSL wind log (https://psl.noaa.gov/boulder/wind.html) lists **54 January high-wind events across 24 years**, including these with gusts of 100 mph or more:
  - Jan 15–16, 1967: 125 mph at NCAR
  - Jan 7–8, 1969: 130 mph; "25 roofs blown off"
  - Jan 24–25, 1970: 122 mph
  - Jan 23, 1971: 147 mph at NCAR
  - Jan 16–17, 1982: 137 mph; "40% of all Boulder buildings damaged"
  - Jan 23, 1988: 105 mph; windows broken, power out
  - Jan 9, 1989: 115 mph
  - Jan 24, 1992: 143 mph (the log notes this "may be in knots")
  - Jan 7, 2007: 77–115 mph; "Many highways closed. Blowing/drifting snow"
- **Building risk tied to wind.** Chautauqua's CEO said of the old auditorium before reinforcement, "the structural engineer… said this place could get blown over by a really stiff wind" (BRL, May 28, 2026).
- **City snow response** (https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/snow-and-ice-response; https://bouldercolorado.gov/news/boulder-gets-ready-winter-second-season-storm-size-response-framework, via search):
  - First-priority streets are high-traffic roads, emergency routes and high-ridership transit routes.
  - In "medium" storms (3–8 in), second-priority streets are plowed too: the remaining transit routes, busy alleys, and grades above 6.5%.
  - Residential streets come later.
  - Property owners must clear sidewalks within 24 hours.
- **RTD in snow.** Typical delays are **10–30 min for buses** and 5–15 min for rail (https://www.rtd-denver.com/community/news/2024/rtd-customers-should-plan-for-delays-this-evening-as-the-snow-continues). A "snow desk" activates when 5 buses report stuck. Service is suspended if "buses are getting stuck faster than they are being recovered." Articulated buses can be swapped for shorter ones. On high-wind days the GS route on CO-93 can be rerouted when sustained winds exceed 45 mph (https://www.rtd-denver.com/inside-connections/weathering-the-storm---how-rtd-respons-to-different-severe-weather-events).
- **US-36 and roads.** CDOT maintains US-36. The search found no CDOT festival-specific winter plan (UNCONFIRMED). Use CDOT COtrip for live conditions.
- **Micromobility cutoff.** The festival says scooters and e-bikes work "unless there is more than a foot of snow" (Attend in Person).
- **Outdoor queues.** Map amenities show Chautauqua as outdoor queue only, with no indoor waiting. Casey, the Boulder Theater, Old Main, Macky and eTown also list outdoor queues.
